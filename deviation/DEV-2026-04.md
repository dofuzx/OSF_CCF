## **Summary of Deviation**

The M2 integration quality gate was not passed: kBET <0.3 did not meet the requirement, and the pre-registration hypothesis regarding kBET failure was primarily attributable to confounding arising from cross-organizational comparisons; this hypothesis was not supported. Residual batch effects may stem from differences in capture chemistry (c295v4 vs. v2 vs. v3 across the 10x versions), sequencing depth, and the quality-control standards used in the various studies; furthermore, embedding-space integration cannot correct for compositional differences.

## Details of the Deviation

According to the M2 quality gate criteria specified in the registration-locking content, the average kBET rejection rate for myeloid cells after Harmony integration should be <0.3; this serves as an entry criterion for downstream clustering annotation and FOLR2+ TAM identification. The observed results after actual implementation were as follows: across 7 datasets (GSE178341/132465/144735/146771/231559/245552/192935), comprising a total of 112,210 myeloid cells, kBET was calculated after Harmony integration (batch = dataset, 30 PCs) using a sampling approach of 1,000 cells per sample: the average rejection rate was 0.631 (expected value = 0.055, permutation p <0.001), far exceeding the 0.3 threshold. This indicates that residual batch structure is significantly present among the datasets.

Sensitivity validation: after excluding the sole non-colorectal cancer dataset, GSE192935 (breast cancer), the kBET value for the CRC-only dataset (6 datasets) was 0.630, nearly identical to the value obtained with the full dataset. This suggests that the residual batch structure is primarily present among the CRC datasets and cannot be attributed to differences in tissue origin, thereby ruling out the explanation that "differences in true composition confound kBET."

## Impact Assessment

1.The FOLR2+ TAM identification (C1) and signature derivation (C2) were performed in the integrated space and may be affected by residual batch effects;

2.FOLR2+ TAM was independently detected across all seven datasets (proportion: 0.6%–4.7%; median: 2.5%), with no evidence of dataset-specific dominance;

3.The top 30 genes in the C2 signature (1,708 genes; Wilcoxon FDR <0.01; log₂FC> 0.5) included typical tissue-resident macrophage markers such as LYVE1, FOLR2, VCAM1, CD5L, CLEC4G, CCL13, F13A1, and EGFL7; no genes were specific to any single dataset;

4.The C2 signature overlapped with the FOLR2+ reference signature from the GSE192935 breast cancer dataset in 22 genes, with a hypergeometric p-value of q = 6.5 × 10⁻²⁵ (pre-registration threshold: q <0.05; passed). These results indicate that this test is based on gene sets rather than embedding space and is insensitive to batch structure.

## Resolution

1. Retain the results of the Harmony integration and do not change the integration method afterward (to avoid overintegration, which could erase the true heterogeneity of TAMs in tissues, and to prevent threshold shopping).

2. Use three layers of alternative quality evidence to support the downstream M2 conclusions:

 (i) the reproducibility table for FOLR2+ TAMs across the 7/7 datasets (M2b_folr2tam_by_dataset.csv);

 (ii) the C2 signature hypergeometric enrichment test, q = 6.5 × 10⁻²⁵;

(iii) the C2 top 30 markers are consistent with the literature-reported tissue-resident macrophage lineage.

The known limitation kBET = 0.631 will be included in the M2 output and the final report under the "Limitations" section. For M4 (ML signature) and M5 (mediation analysis), signature scores are used at the sample level and are less affected by batch effects at the cellular level.

The M7 robustness module will include a "leave-one-dataset-out" re-evaluation of the stability of the C2 signature (the original plan called for leaving out only one cohort in the metagenomics portion); if the signature reverses direction after being left out in any dataset, this deviation will be traced back and the integration strategy will be re-evaluated.
